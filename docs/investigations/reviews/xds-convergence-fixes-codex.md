## Findings in the revision

1. **P1 · architecture · Q1 and §4.1 — A projection of key, type and version cannot support the promised verdict.** Q1 requires named-subscription coverage, a binding to the serving controller replica and key, a comparable capture window, and evidence of a hold. The projection drops resource membership. `/snapshots/xds` reads the embedded cache, which can expose a new version before the corresponding serving cache is installed. The withheld gauge covers first publish at Gateway scope, leaving a per-key flip hold invisible to this join. Even with U9, equal snapshot versions can coexist with held intent, and agreement among fetched keys only helps once those keys are known to cover the pod’s serving connection. E14 needs to retain the required evidence or explicitly scope when Q1 stays `unknown`. 2. **P1 · correctness · Q3 — A cumulative reject counter cannot establish a rejection after the last acceptance.** In S7, `q8vvd` has accepted the repaired RDS version and still reports 14 rejects for `8080` and 7 for `8081`. Envoy advances the acceptance timestamp on success without resetting the rejection counter. Its SotW mux also notifies every watch of the type when response processing throws, so a watch’s increment cannot identify which resource caused the NACK. A single scrape supports “a rejection was observed.” A same-process interval with a rising reject counter and an unchanged success counter can establish ordering. Misreading S7 would report a rejection after recovery. 3. **P2 · architecture · §4.2 U7 — The proposed record needs to distinguish stream/type evidence from a subscription outcome.** One SotW RDS response can contain several named resources. Envoy dispatches them to watches sequentially, and one callback can succeed before another fails. The last-sent nonce plus version-only ACK/NACK fields also loses the event-to-response link once a newer response is sent. Retain nonce and time on each correlated outcome, identify the response’s resource scope, and define what a newer publication or stream close does to the current-rejection answer. A stream/type record can establish that a response was NACKed. A per-watch outcome still needs proxy evidence.
